Know Before You Marry

Twenty-four things worth understanding—and talking about—before you make a lifelong promise.

September 24, 2026 · 8 min read · Basanta Saru

A wedding is one day. Marriage is the life you build afterward: ordinary mornings, difficult conversations, small kindnesses, and choices you make again and again. Before you marry, talk honestly about what that life might look like. These are the lessons I would want to remember.

01 Love is not enough on its own

Love may bring two people together, but habits and commitment help them stay together. Feelings change. On days when affection feels quieter, patience and the willingness to keep showing up matter.

02 Respect matters more than romance

Romance can make a relationship exciting; respect makes it feel safe. Notice how you speak to each other when you disagree. Can you still admire this person when you are angry?

03 Communication is essential

Talk about money, emotions, family, and expectations before marriage. Silence can let small misunderstandings grow. Honest conversations now make it easier to face difficult ones later.

04 Choose character over chemistry

Attraction matters, but so do integrity, patience, empathy, and consistency. Notice who someone is when there is nothing to impress and no reward for doing the right thing.

05 Marriage is daily work

A promise made at a wedding has to be lived in thousands of ordinary mornings. Commitment means making time, taking responsibility, and caring for each other even when it is inconvenient.

06 Do not marry someone's potential

Believe what you see today. People can grow, but marriage is not a plan for changing another person. Ask yourself whether you can accept their present habits and character.

07 Emotional safety comes first

Can you be vulnerable, cry, admit a mistake, or talk about pain without fearing ridicule? Physical attraction cannot replace the comfort of being understood and treated gently.

08 Do not compete with your partner

Marriage is a partnership, not a scoreboard. Celebrate each other's successes and face setbacks as a team. You both live with the consequences of how you treat one another.

09 You marry their habits, not their highlights

Look beyond the best dates. How do they treat service workers, respond to stress, handle a disappointment, and speak about people who are absent? Everyday behavior tells you more than charm does.

10 Shared values matter more than shared interests

Enjoying the same music is lovely. Agreement—or an honest way to navigate differences—on values, life goals, family, and beliefs has a deeper effect on the life you will build.

11 Talk openly about money

Discuss debt, spending, saving, responsibilities, and financial goals. Hidden debts and secret spending erode trust. You do not need to have everything figured out, but you do need honesty.

12 Forgive, and learn

Holding on to every small hurt makes closeness difficult. Apologize, repair what you can, and learn from the conflict. Forgiveness does not require ignoring repeated harm or abandoning your boundaries.

13 Arguments are not the problem; disrespect is

Two people will disagree. What matters is how they disagree: without insults, threats, or trying to dominate. The aim is understanding and repair, not victory.

14 Make room for an honest apology

Pride can keep a small mistake alive for days. Saying “I was wrong” and changing your behavior is more useful than defending yourself at every cost. Both people deserve accountability.

15 Protect your privacy without isolating yourself

Talk to your spouse about problems instead of turning every disagreement into a public debate. Trusted friends, family, or professional support can still help when you need perspective or safety.

16 Keep dating each other

Marriage does not end romance; neglect can make it fade. Make time for walks, conversations, shared meals, and the small gestures that helped you feel close in the beginning.

17 Choose someone who works through problems with you

Life will bring enough challenges of its own. Look for a partner who can face a problem with you, take responsibility, and seek solutions rather than make every difficult moment harder.

18 Loyalty is more than fidelity

It is keeping promises, being honest when nobody is watching, and speaking about your partner with care. Loyalty includes protecting trust, not simply avoiding betrayal.

19 Boundaries keep love healthy

Closeness and individuality can coexist. Talk about privacy, time alone, friendships, family involvement, and what each of you needs to feel respected.

20 You do not have to win every argument

Some disagreements need a decision; others need understanding. Let go of the urge to prove a minor point, while still speaking up about what genuinely matters to you.

21 Care for emotional and physical intimacy

Feeling safe, wanted, and heard helps many couples stay close physically. Ask what each person needs, listen to their boundaries, and keep the conversation open as those needs change.

22 Do not expect marriage to heal every wound

A partner can support you, but they cannot do all your healing for you. Loneliness, anger, and insecurity deserve attention before and during marriage. Seek help when you need it.

23 Grow together on purpose

People change over time. Keep asking each other what matters now, what you are learning, and where you hope to go. Shared growth takes curiosity rather than an assumption that you will always want the same things.

24 Keep humor alive

Laughing together can soften a hard day. It does not solve every problem, but a little playfulness can remind you that you are still on the same side.

Choose the person you can build an ordinary life with—and become someone who can build one with them.

None of these points is a guarantee of a perfect marriage. They are invitations to ask better questions before promising a shared future, and to keep asking them after the wedding day.
