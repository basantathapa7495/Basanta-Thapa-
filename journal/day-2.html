01

THE DAY AS IT HAPPENED

Messy, but moving.

8:00AM
A slower start

I missed my 5 AM wake-up.

My alarm rang at 5 AM, but after all the walking from the previous day, my body felt tired. I went back to sleep and woke up around 8 AM. It was not the start I wanted, but I decided not to treat the whole day as ruined.

Brushed & washed upNam JapLight meal
Morning
Building in public

I worked on the Age 20 section of my website.

I spent the morning using ChatGPT and working on the Age 20 part of my website. The page was messy at first, but I kept experimenting instead of waiting for the perfect plan.

Imperfect work is still work you can improve.

10:00AM
Documenting the journey

I turned Day 1 into something I could share.

I wrote my Day 1 journal, which took about an hour. Then I used AI to summarize it, remove private details, and turn the story into a structured YouTube script.

That was an important shift for me: I was not only living these days anymore—I was documenting them.

Midday
Create and publish

I recorded, edited, and uploaded my Day 1 video.

I read the script from my laptop while speaking to the camera, edited the video in CapCut, and uploaded it to YouTube. I am still learning how to speak naturally on camera, but publishing was more important than waiting until I felt perfect.

RecordedEdited in CapCutPublished
Afternoon
Low energy

I kept feeling sleepy and unfocused.

I took a short nap, ate another light meal, and later tried to nap again for only a few minutes. That nap became much longer than planned. I also noticed that I did not have a clear priority for the day, so I kept jumping between fixing things.

“Being busy and making progress are not always the same thing.”
Later
Trying to learn

I stepped away from the laptop and tried to read.

I tried reading a book, but the vocabulary felt difficult and I had to keep looking up meanings. It slowed me down, but it also reminded me that difficulty is part of learning.

Evening
Resetting the work

Going back helped me move forward.

My recent website code had become messy, so instead of forcing more fixes on top of it, I restored an older version. That gave me a cleaner base and made the project much easier to control again.

Starting again from a better foundation is not failure.

Sunset
Away from the screen

I went outside and watched some volleyball.

I went out with a friend, rode around for a while, and stopped where people were playing volleyball. I played a little and mostly watched. Seeing how much younger people around me had grown reminded me how quickly time moves.

Night
Back into flow

I ended the day working better than I started it.

After dinner, I went back to my room and continued fixing my website. This time the work started flowing. I managed to fix around half of what I wanted and kept going until about 11 PM.

The day was not organized, but it was not wasted.

02

WHAT DAY TWO TAUGHT ME

Progress can look messy.

01

A late start is not the end

Waking up later than planned does not mean the rest of the day has to be thrown away.

02

Publish before perfect

I learned more by recording and uploading than I would have learned by waiting until I felt ready.

03

Choose the next useful thing

When I feel lost, I need a clear next action instead of switching endlessly between small tasks.

04

Reset when the foundation is messy

Restoring an older version of my website made forward progress easier than forcing more fixes into broken code.

A BETTER WAY TO MEASURE DAY TWO

I felt lost, but I still created evidence of progress.

The day did not feel highly productive, yet several meaningful things moved forward.

  • ✓ Wrote my Day 1 journal
  • ✓ Created a YouTube script
  • ✓ Recorded, edited, and uploaded a video
  • ✓ Worked on my website
  • ✓ Tried to read and learn
  • ✓ Went outside and moved around
  • ✓ Restored a cleaner website version

THE RULE FOR TOMORROW

Start with a clear plan.

I do not need to know everything I will do. I just need to know what matters most and what the next useful action is.
