01

THE DAY AS IT HAPPENED

Not perfect. Still meaningful.

5:00AM
A quiet start

I started the morning with intention.

I brushed my teeth, washed my face, made my bed, and placed a God wallpaper sticker on the wall. Then I sat down for ten minutes of meditation. Nothing dramatic—just a calm beginning to a new year of my life.

Made my bed10 min meditationMorning shower
7:30AM
Plans changed

Real life interrupted the routine.

I had received unexpected news the day before, so I needed to leave home early. I skipped my planned workout—and that turned out to be the right choice. By the end of the day, I had walked for more than two hours.

Sometimes adapting is wiser than forcing the plan.

DAYTIME
Small decisions

I chose control instead of an extreme promise.

Over the last year, I gained weight because I was eating too much. On my birthday, I decided to begin simply: eat a little less and eat with more awareness. I also stayed away from Shorts and Reels, kept my phone out of the toilet, and put rubbish where it belonged.

These choices look small from the outside. But repeated choices become a lifestyle.

5:00PM
PRIVATE The unexpected lesson

Some people reunited after a long misunderstanding.

I won’t share the details publicly. What matters is what I learned: relationships are complicated, silence can create years of distance, and conversations can begin to repair what misunderstanding broke.

“Sometimes the biggest lesson of the day is the part you cannot put on camera.”
EVENING
The walk home

One simple hour with my dad felt important.

When everything was finished, my dad and I walked home together for about an hour. It was Father’s Day too, which made that ordinary walk feel quietly meaningful. We ate when we got home, and by then I was completely exhausted.

I had planned to video call my uncle, but I didn’t have the energy. The day was full, imperfect, and real.
